# Characters and working poses

Authored drawing guidance, distilled from primary artist lessons checked on 2026-10-09. The construction and correction exercises below are our practical application, not a mandatory anatomy standard or a copied tutorial. They work without access to the linked pages. Stylized, realistic, tiny, and deliberately rigid characters need different treatment.

## Explore the focal silhouette

For important unfamiliar person or creature, record intended occupied pixel footprint and explore several materially different flat silhouettes before committing. Roughly three candidates are often enough to expose weak first idea without turning exploration into quota. Change structural choices—body rhythm, head-to-body ratio, stance, support, near/far limb separation, appendage mass, and negative spaces—not merely colors or accessories.

Select silhouette that best communicates identity and gesture at native delivery size. Then establish head, chest, pelvis, near and far limbs, support/contact, and any defining appendage before clothing or texture. Tiny stylized design may merge masses, but viewer should not have to guess whether merged shape is torso, pelvis, or hind limb.

## Establish a person before a costume

A sprite's rectangle is not the person's proportions. Decide the occupied height, head-to-body relationship, and view. A big head can improve expression in a tiny character; a smaller head can suit a taller, more realistic design. Do not impose an eight-head or six-head rule on every sprite.

Start with the head, chest, pelvis, and limb directions. A rough line through the spine can suggest the movement connecting them. Then give the chest and pelvis width and depth. Decide which shoulder, arm, and leg are nearer the viewer. A consistent three-quarter view can legitimately show unequal sides; identical rectangles on both sides are not automatically clearer. [Raymond Schlitter / Slynyrd: anatomy and gesture](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2019/5/21/pixelblog-17-human-anatomy).

For an attentive worker, try a small head turn toward the task and a chest position that supports the reach. Draw the coat over that structure: shoulder slope, waist, hem, and sleeve opening should follow the body. Rectangles and polygons are useful construction tools, but adding pockets to an unreadable torso does not repair the pose. Shading cannot compensate for a weak underlying design. [Derek Yu: common mistakes](https://derekyu.com/makegames/pixelart2.html).

## Make the face point somewhere

Treat the skull and hair as large shapes before individual strands. Place visible features for the chosen view: in side view, the nose, brow, and chin affect the contour; in front or three-quarter view, feature spacing and the nearer eye matter. Tiny sprites may communicate attention through the head outline and a few deliberate pixels rather than detailed facial anatomy. [Slynyrd: faces and hair](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2020/7/28/pixelblog-29-anime-faces-and-hair).

Our review method: view the head at the intended size and ask where the character looks. When goggles are required, decide whether they are separate lenses or an opaque visor. Preserve an intended visor; do not remove it merely to reveal eyes. If it unintentionally hides all expression, adjust the lens separation, brow, head angle, or supporting body language. A large blue patch is not a substitute for this choice.

## Draw the action through the body

Gesture describes the movement connecting body parts, not every small contour. Decide what the character is doing before choosing a hand coordinate. [Proko: drawing gesture](https://static.proko.com/static/course-lesson/how-to-draw-gesture).

For a reach or pour, consider:

- Where is the weight supported: feet, a seat, or a leaning surface?
- Which way do the chest and head turn toward the vessel?
- How do shoulder, elbow, wrist, and grip connect?
- Does the hand meet the bottle at a believable grip rather than float next to it?
- Can the viewer distinguish the arm, bottle, and torso at native size?

These are visual checks, not requirements that every action move every body part. A restrained cutout animation or intentionally robotic worker can be correct. Foreshortening changes projected limb length; smears and deliberate stretching can serve fast action. Review whether the change reads as intentional rather than enforcing equal pixel-length limbs in all frames. [Slynyrd: action exaggeration](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/9/8/pixelblog-9-melee-attacks).

## Inspect and correct the draft

Render the whole character with the relevant prop and surface before making many variants. Inspect the silhouette (the outer shape filled with one color), a few light/dark masses, the native-size face, and an enlarged view. Temporary flat-color construction can make near/far limbs easier to distinguish; it is a drafting aid, not necessarily the final palette.

Name the mismatch before revising. If the hand disappears against the coat, separate the shapes or adjust their values before adding finger pixels. If head and torso appear to face unrelated directions, review their connection before changing the hair. If the pose reads as standing rather than working, revise the weight, chest, head, and grip together where needed. Keep already accepted design decisions and user edits intact.
