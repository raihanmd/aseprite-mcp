# Scenes, perspective, and material separation

Authored guidance derived from primary artist lessons checked on 2026-10-09. The scene exercises are our application, usable offline. They do not mandate a particular palette, projection, lighting direction, or level of realism.

## Make the subject win before adding texture

Block the scene's major masses first: focal subject, supporting objects or terrain, and background. An environment may have no actor; an action scene may need a character, prop, and working surface together. Inspect the assembled layout at the intended size. Decide where the viewer should look. Reserve useful separation in silhouette, brightness, or color for that area; maximum contrast everywhere leaves nothing visually important.

Texture can use characteristic shapes, variation, and quieter regions rather than equally outlining every brick, board, or hair. Distant areas can use less contrast and detail where that supports depth, without mechanically adding outdoor haze to every room. [Slynyrd: texture](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/2/15/pixelblog-2-texture), [landscape depth](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/11/16/pixelblog-11-landscape-pixeling).

For a workshop or laboratory, show the character, held object, and working surface before filling shelves. For a landscape, establish the focal landform or route before decorating every tree. If an important shape disappears into a busy background, simplify or change that local separation. If a foreground object hides the action, revise overlap or placement. Detail should support the focal subject.

## Choose coherent viewing rules

Establish how surfaces and volumes are viewed: front, side, three-quarter, top-down, or isometric. A stylized projection may trade physical accuracy for consistency. Use the same viewing rules for related scene objects unless a deliberate mixed treatment is part of the brief. A common two-across/one-up isometric step is one convention, not a requirement for every scene. [Slynyrd: graphical projections](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/3/14/pixelblog-3-graphical-projections-1), [isometric projection](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/4/12/pixelblog-4-graphical-projection-part-2).

Our check: compare the bench top, bottle opening, shelves, and character's visible sides. Do they imply compatible viewing directions? Separate construction guides from final outlines; the grid should assist the view rather than make every object a front-facing rectangle.

## Shade the form, then signal the material

Choose light direction, the object's major faces or rounded volume, and its own shaded regions first. A cast shadow belongs on the receiving surface. Glossy highlights and matte shading serve different purposes; tiny art cannot carry every physical effect. [Saint11: basic shading](https://saint11.art/pixel_art_articles/article4/).

Our optional material study uses a few small objects under the same light:

- **Fabric:** larger light/shadow masses following the body and major folds. A sleeve crease should relate to the elbow; it need not have a white glossy stripe.
- **Glass and liquid:** a readable glass boundary or reflection plus a separate fill level and liquid volume. Keep the background/transparency treatment intentional. A chosen clear reflection does not require physically simulated refraction.
- **Metal:** a small strong reflection or abrupt value change can suggest a polished surface; rough metal can use a different treatment.
- **Wood:** establish plane brightness before restrained grain. Repeated dark grain on every surface can compete with the subject.

These are design options, not compulsory recipes. Flat or monochrome artwork remains valid when it matches the brief. Review whether each cue explains a surface at native size, not how many effects were added. Light direction and contrast can also set mood. [Slynyrd: light and shadow](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/6/15/pixelblog-6-light-and-shadow).

## Interpret angular or faceted style visibly

The harness translates "high poly" and "low poly" in pixel-art requests to detail/rendering density; those labels alone do not request angular shapes. Use this section when the user actually wants faceted or angular pixel art. For 2D faceted pixel art, place distinct light/shadow patches according to the planes of the head, coat, vessel, or furniture. A diagonal patch should describe a face of the volume, not be random triangular decoration. Angular shading can coexist with expressive contours and an attentive pose. An explicit rendered low-poly 3D request is a separate production requirement; do not call a pixel drawing a 3D model.

Before polishing, inspect the composition with few colors, then with the intended materials. If it reads only when enlarged, strengthen the relevant large shape or separation. For transparent assets, inspect edges against plausible backgrounds. Correct one visible issue at a time and preserve accepted objects and user edits.
