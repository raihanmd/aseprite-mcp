# Pixel-art source map and study order

Checked **2026-10-09**. These are original artist and educator pages, not scraped tutorial copies. The [research guide](pixel-art-deep.md) explains the conclusions and the maintained [art skill](../../skills/aseprite-pixel-art/SKILL.md) turns selected principles into working instructions.

## Reading scope

- **Text:** the relevant tutorial/lesson prose was read. Images linked from the page are not automatically evidence that their diagrams or animation were visually studied.
- **Indexed text:** the lesson's search-indexed body was available and read; direct opening was unavailable or not separately verified. More limited than a reliable full-page retrieval.
- **Overview:** selected descriptive/documentation sections were read, not the entire collection or a compatibility test.
- **Catalog:** useful for finding future lessons, not evidence that every listed tutorial, video or book was studied.

Tutorial GIF playback was not reviewed. The remote image-viewing route was unavailable; no claim relies on visual inspection of those diagrams. Local trial PNGs were inspected separately. Publication dates are included only where confirmed in the retrieved page; all rows share the access/check date above. URL date slugs sometimes differ from the displayed date, so slugs are not treated as publication evidence.

## Suggested study order for this project

1. **Core drawing:** Saint11 clusters/shading, Derek Yu's construction and critique, then Cure's terminology. The goal is to understand shapes, not memorize bans.
2. **The weak case:** Slynyrd anatomy and faces, plus Proko gesture. Establish a readable scientist before polishing its costume.
3. **Motion:** Slynyrd key poses and body-first walk workflow; Toon Boom's preparation, timing, paths and follow-through.
4. **Scene:** coherent projection, quiet/background texture and tile boundaries.
5. **Delivery:** scaling/consistency, actual output inspection, and only then optional 3D-reference or alternative production trials.

This order is our curriculum proposal, not an artist-endorsed AI training program. Material-specific graphic lessons and real motion references should be inspected when a concrete asset needs them. Video-only, inaccessible and paid material remains further study; more links are not a substitute for validating the next artwork.

## Access, source handling and stopping condition

The offline research helper failed before gathering sources because its dependency cache was incomplete. Direct primary-source web research supplied this pass instead. Some direct lesson pages were blocked; search-indexed text is labeled above. The remote tutorial-image browser was unavailable and GIF retrieval was unsupported in an attempted example. No dependencies, browser packages, paid courses or new tools were installed to work around those restrictions.

Store these bounded, authored conclusions and URLs, not full copyrighted tutorials or example assets. For an eventual open-source skill, keep our own instructions separate from third-party artwork and check reuse rights before bundling any reference images.

The stopping condition for this pass is coverage of the major drawing, animation, scene and display problems with explicit source limits. It is not a claim of reading every pixel-art tutorial on the internet or proof that the revised AI workflow will succeed. That proof needs the next matched creation/revision trial after user review.
